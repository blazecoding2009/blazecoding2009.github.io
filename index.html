About

I work at the seam between mechanical and electrical, where a decision on one side tends to break something on the other.

Oakville, Ontario

I build things. Most of my time goes into hands-on engineering — designing and machining parts for a competition electric race car, leading electrical on an FRC robot, and iterating drone hardware through three full hardware cycles. I was the first student on my FRC team certified on the Tormach 1100MX CNC mill, and I spent a summer at DRDO developing autonomous drone software with ROS2.

Working across both disciplines means I spend a lot of time at the intersection, where a design decision on the mechanical side breaks something electrical, or the reverse. That's the part I find most interesting.

Outside of build season I organize hackathons, mentor FLL teams, and hold a Training Petty Officer role in the Royal Canadian Sea Cadets, where I've placed top in my phase four years running.

Warrig X2 — Electrathon Race Car

2025 — Present
Role

Vehicle
Single-seat electric

Single-seat electric race car competing in the Electrathon Canada series. I lead mechanical and electrical — the rear drivetrain redesign was the focus of the 2025 season.

  • Replaced a failing brushed single-motor system with three Kraken X60 brushless motors sharing a West Coast Products gearbox at an 11.46:1 total reduction (12:60 internal stage, 24:55 chain stage).
  • Designed the motor-mount bracket through a full iteration cycle — a 3D-printed PLA prototype exposed a structural failure in the bolt pattern, so I redesigned and had the final version machined from aluminum.
  • Owned CAN bus layout and per-motor current limiting via Talon FX telemetry.
  • Authored the full Engineering Design Report submitted to competition.
